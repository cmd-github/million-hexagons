# Million Hexagons: pre-launch legal review

**Research date: 29 September 2026.** This is a research checklist and drafting brief, not a legal opinion. I have treated **CRAIG-CONFIRMED-DECISIONS-AND-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md** as the current product direction. The draft commercial terms’ aspiration that purchases become non-cancellable when publication begins is **not yet an established legal outcome**. The launch acceptance document correctly leaves legal wording, country scope and live checkout evidence open.

The central question for a solicitor is what the customer buys. The initial published artwork may be **digital content**, but the continuing allocation of particular cells, public hosting, editing and availability look strongly like an **ongoing digital or advertising service**. It may be a mixed contract. Calling the cells “owned” or calling Stripe the Merchant of Record does not settle either issue. A consent checkbox may remove a cooling-off right for qualifying digital content; it does **not** necessarily remove the cooling-off right for an ongoing service merely because its first page appears immediately. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

## A. Launch-blocking decisions, in priority order

1. **Settle contract classification and UK cancellation design with a consumer solicitor.** Until then, do not tell consumers that all purchases are final on publication. Design the order and fulfilment records to support either a valid digital-content waiver or a service cancellation with any lawful proportionate charge.
2. **Choose and clear the actual launch countries.** “Eurozone” and “other supported countries” are pricing groups, not legal clearance. Start with an explicit country allow-list and obtain country-specific advice before accepting consumers in each non-UK country.
3. **Identify every contracting party and customer document.** Inspect the actual Stripe/OneLink checkout, terms, receipt, invoice, Link account view and refund path. Resolve who contracts for the placement service, who takes payment, who receives a cancellation or defect claim, and who issues each refund. Stripe has answered the conditional UK-customer VAT question; the accountant still needs to determine the actual invoice and payout treatment.
4. **Approve the duration, closure and remedy terms.** “For as long as Million Hexagons operates” needs a fair closure mechanism, a real remedy for premature or avoidable cessation, and no suggestion of perpetual property.
5. **Assess the UK Online Safety Act before reactive publication goes live.** Customer artwork and links publicly encountered by other users make the user-to-user definition a serious possibility. Document scope, risk assessment, children’s access assessment, reporting and applicable safeguards; commission specialist advice on exemptions and duties. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}
6. **Finish the privacy and tracking audit.** In particular, public placement view/click counts may be advertising-performance measurement. Do not classify their device storage as exempt “site analytics” without examining its purpose and implementation. The UK’s newer statistical-purpose exception is narrow and requires an easy objection route. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

### Practical handling of the common failure cases

- **Mistaken purchase:** offer a prompt support route; apply the actual statutory cancellation position first. A goodwill policy can be more generous.
- **Duplicate charge or allocation:** investigate and reverse the unintended transaction; prevent the same event creating a second claim. Resolve the affected cells explicitly.
- **Paid but unpublished:** show a recoverable status, keep the precise cells reserved, retry and notify the customer. If it cannot be delivered, provide the promised full-refund route and decide entitlement/cell release in the same resolution.
- **Extended outage or faulty editing/rendering:** investigate, restore or repeat performance where possible, then consider a statutory price reduction or other remedy. A broad “availability not guaranteed” clause will not erase the core obligation.
- **Closure:** give meaningful advance notice where possible, preserve account access and a record of claims during wind-down, and apply the solicitor-approved remedy. Fraud, unlawful content and serious owner breach can justify different action, but must be narrowly drafted and evidenced.

### Content policy and complaint workflow

A concise policy should cover **rights to submitted material; prohibited unlawful and harmful content; impersonation and misleading claims; malware/phishing and unsafe destinations; adult and child sexual content; hate and threats; personal data and privacy; how reports are assessed; available actions; owner notices; correction and appeal; repeat abuse; and law-enforcement preservation/escalation**. Do not promise that every upload is checked before publication.

Give visitors one conspicuous **“Report this placement”** route. Collect the cell/placement URL, the alleged issue, supporting details and a reply address; avoid demanding unnecessary identity evidence. Triage imminent harm and clearly illegal material urgently. Record the version, report, evidence, decision, reason, operator and times; disable a dangerous link or content promptly while preserving the claimed state where appropriate. Notify the owner with enough detail to respond unless doing so would create a safety or legal problem. Allow correction and a human appeal. Escalate repeat substantiated infringement or abuse from warning to edit restriction, suspension and, for serious or persistent cases, revocation. The precise notice, complaint and records obligations depend on the OSA/DSA scope decision. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="6"}

### Review page: purchase description

**Draft for solicitor review**

### Review page: immediate-supply control and nearby explanation

**Draft for solicitor review — conditional wording requiring classification-specific revision**

> ☐ **I expressly request that Million Hexagons start supplying my placement as soon as payment succeeds, before any applicable 14-day cancellation period ends. I understand that starting supply may affect my statutory cancellation rights as explained below.**

> **What this means:** Your claim and placement are intended to become available shortly after successful payment. If the law treats part of your order as immediately supplied digital content, you may lose the right to cancel **that part** once supply begins, if the legal conditions are met. That is an **honest interim explanation**, not a guaranteed waiver mechanism. ## E. Facts that could materially change the advice

1. **Which exact countries** will be allowed on day one, and will you actively market to consumers in their languages or territories?
2. Can **under-18s browse** the public globe or create accounts/purchase, and will any age control exist?
3. What exactly is promised at launch for **availability, edits, permanent URLs and duration**? Is any minimum operating period commercially acceptable to you?
4. What **public name** appears: a chosen placement title only, a separate owner display name, or the buyer’s legal name? Can the owner remove both from the public page and search results?
5. Do placement view/click counts use **cookies, local storage, device identifiers, IP-based deduplication or third-party scripts**, and are they presented to purchasers as advertising performance?
6. Which **direct email and geographic business/complaints address** will Birdcage publish, after checking the current registered-office change?
7. In the configured Stripe flow, who do the **checkout terms, receipt, invoice and refund screen** name as the seller of the placement and the contact for statutory claims?
8. Will you allow political advertising, regulated products, adult material, gambling, or health/financial claims? These choices materially affect content and country review.
9. Can an owner **hide all public content while keeping cells claimed**, and will a cancelled/refunded consumer claim be released for resale? The legal and technical flow must agree.

I would take the classification, cancellation and country list to the solicitor first. Those answers determine the checkout copy and whether the intended immediate-publication flow can launch as designed.
