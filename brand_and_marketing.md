# Million Hexagons brand guidance

## Positioning

Million Hexagons is one shared, explorable 3D canvas containing exactly 1,000,000 finite claimable spaces. People and businesses can use connected areas for logos, advertising, artwork, messages, and links.

Internal shorthand: “A million scarce spaces on one permanent shared digital canvas.” Use this as a positioning guide, not mandatory copy.

Lead with:

1. There are only 1,000,000 spaces.
2. Anyone can put something of their own on the canvas.
3. A claimed placement remains claimed, subject to product terms and moderation.
4. The shared canvas becomes more interesting as it fills.

The product is advertising inventory, creative expression, scarce internet space, and an internet experiment—not merely an ad platform, a game, or a financial asset.

## Audience and tone

Launch copy should appeal first to internet-native individuals, creators, communities, small businesses, and startups while remaining credible to larger brands.

Use short, direct language that feels bold, minimal, internet-native, slightly playful, visually premium, and commercially credible. Avoid corporate jargon, manufactured hype, fake urgency, nostalgia, childishness, and crypto/NFT language.

Good territory includes “Claim your space,” “Make your mark,” “Create a placement,” and “One million spaces. That’s all there will be.” These are directions, not mandatory production copy.

Prefer `claim`, `placement`, `space`, `hexagons`, and `place on the canvas`. Use `own` only when supported by the product and legal terms.

## Product representation

The globe is a recognisable shared object and a central visual hook. It is not Earth and does not imply geographic ownership. Recognisable prototype brands must be labelled as samples, never customers or endorsements.

Current standard prices are fixed by customer region: £1 per hexagon in the UK, €1 in the eurozone, and $2 in the USA. Do not invent dynamic, auction, or premium-location pricing.

“Permanent” describes the claimed placement, not immutable artwork or an unconditional forever-service guarantee. Product terms must define duration, permitted updates, moderation, and operating commitments before paid launch.

## Claims guardrail

Never promise guaranteed traffic, impressions, returns, appreciation, resale value, unsupported ownership rights, immutable artwork, or unconfirmed pricing. Do not imply that sample brands are customers.

Before publishing copy, confirm that it communicates finite space, what the customer can place, accurate permanence, and the current prototype or purchase status without sounding like generic startup marketing.
