## Million Hexagons — Brand & Marketing Positioning

### Core proposition

Million Hexagons is a shared 3D digital canvas made from exactly 1,000,000 claimable hexagonal spaces.

People and businesses can claim connected areas and use them for:

- logos
- advertising
- artwork
- images
- words and messages
- links to websites or other destinations

The product should not be described as "just advertising".

It sits between:

- permanent digital space
- advertising inventory
- creative expression
- scarce internet real estate
- an internet experiment people can become part of

A useful internal shorthand is:

> A million scarce spaces on one permanent shared digital canvas.

Do not use this exact sentence everywhere. It is a positioning guide, not mandatory copy.

---

### Permanence

Permanence is one of the strongest parts of the proposition.

A claimed placement remains claimed permanently, subject to platform rules and moderation.

Do not imply that the exact creative can never change.

Owners of a placement may be able to update associated content such as:

- logo / artwork
- URL
- description
- other permitted placement details

Prefer wording such as:

- permanent placement
- claim your space
- your place on the canvas
- once claimed, the space stays claimed

Avoid:

- frozen forever
- immutable forever
- exact artwork can never change

---

### Scarcity

Scarcity is central to the product.

There are only 1,000,000 hexagons.

Once all available space has been claimed, there is no additional canvas.

Marketing should make this genuinely clear rather than manufacture fake urgency.

Good themes:

- finite space
- get in early
- claim your place
- once it's gone, it's gone
- early placements become part of the history of the project

Avoid fake countdowns or invented scarcity.

---

### Audience

The product is intentionally broad.

Potential users include:

- individuals
- creators
- artists
- niche communities
- small businesses
- startups
- established brands
- advertisers

Likely adoption sequence:

1. internet-native individuals, creators and niche communities
2. small businesses and startups
3. larger brands once the canvas has visible activity, social proof and cultural relevance

Marketing at launch should therefore feel accessible and interesting to individuals while still looking credible enough that businesses can imagine participating.

Do not make the brand feel like an enterprise advertising platform.

Do not make it feel like a toy that serious businesses would avoid.

---

### Product identity

The 3D sphere is a major part of the identity.

It should feel like:

- one shared object
- finite
- explorable
- visibly filling up over time
- recognisable at a glance

The sphere is not merely navigation.

It is part of the visual hook and should feature prominently in:

- website imagery
- social posts
- launch graphics
- milestone content
- demonstrations

However, marketing should not imply it represents planet Earth or geographic locations unless that becomes an actual product feature.

Avoid phrases such as:

- part of the world
- advertise on the world
- own a country / location

unless they are literally true.

---

### Emotional drivers

The strongest emotional driver is scarcity / exclusivity.

Secondary drivers are:

- leaving your mark
- being early
- being part of an unusual internet project
- permanent visibility
- creative freedom
- being part of internet history if the project succeeds

The user should ideally understand several things within seconds:

> There are only a million spaces.

> I can put something of mine there.

> It stays claimed.

> Other people and businesses are doing the same thing.

> If this takes off, getting in early matters.

---

### Tone

The exact brand voice is not fully locked yet.

Until it is, use a blend of:

- bold
- minimal
- internet-native
- slightly playful
- confident
- visually premium
- commercially credible

Avoid extremes.

Do not sound:

- overly corporate
- crypto-like
- NFT-like
- aggressively salesy
- childish
- deliberately "random"
- fake-exclusive
- overly nostalgic

Copy should usually be short and direct.

Prefer:

> Claim your space.

over:

> Unlock the next generation of revolutionary digital brand ownership.

Prefer:

> 1,000,000 spaces. That's all there will be.

over manufactured hype.

---

### Ownership language

Prefer:

- claim
- placement
- space
- your hexagons
- your area
- your place on the canvas

Use "own" cautiously unless the product/legal terms explicitly support ownership language.

Do not casually describe hexagons as legal property, land or financial assets.

---

### Pricing

Current intended model:

- primarily a flat price per hexagon
- one-time purchase / placement pricing
- pricing may later vary for premium positions such as visually central areas

The premium-location concept is not yet final.

Agents must not invent:

- a confirmed centre premium
- dynamic pricing
- auction pricing
- specific prices

unless these have been explicitly confirmed in the current product specification.

---

### Million Dollar Homepage reference

The Million Dollar Homepage is an inspiration and useful comparison, but should not dominate the brand.

It may be referenced occasionally where it helps someone instantly understand the idea.

Examples:

- press
- founder story
- explanatory social content
- "inspired by..." context

Avoid making Million Hexagons look like a direct clone.

The product should develop its own identity.

---

### Messaging hierarchy

When writing marketing copy, prioritise these ideas roughly in this order:

1. There are only 1,000,000 spaces.
2. You can claim a space and put something on it.
3. The placement is permanent.
4. It can be used for logos, ads, art, images or messages.
5. The whole thing exists on one shared 3D canvas.
6. Early participation matters because the space is finite.

Not every message needs to communicate all six.

---

### Useful headline territory

Examples of acceptable directions:

- Claim your space.
- One million spaces. That's all there will be.
- Make your mark.
- Get your place before someone else does.
- One shared canvas. One million chances to be part of it.
- Put something here that lasts.
- Logos. Art. Messages. Ads. Your space.
- A million hexagons. What will yours become?
- Get in early. Stay on the canvas.
- Your place on something bigger.

These are directional examples, not mandatory production copy.

---

### Calls to action

Prefer:

- Claim your space
- Create a placement
- Choose your hexagons
- Explore the canvas
- See what's been claimed
- Get in early
- Start your placement

Avoid generic SaaS CTAs where possible:

- Get started
- Learn more
- Unlock now

unless the context genuinely suits them.

---

### Claims and wording to avoid

Do not claim:

- guaranteed advertising exposure
- guaranteed traffic
- investment returns
- appreciating value
- resale value
- ownership rights beyond the actual product terms
- that the sphere is Earth
- that placement artwork is immutable
- that pricing mechanics are final when they are not
- that major brands shown in mockups are actual customers

Any recognisable brands used in prototypes or demonstrations must be clearly understood as sample/demo content, not endorsements or customers.

---

### Brand test

Before approving public-facing copy, ask:

1. Does someone understand there is finite space?
2. Do they understand they can put something of their own on it?
3. Does the permanence come through accurately?
4. Does this sound interesting to both an individual and a business?
5. Are we avoiding claims the actual product cannot yet support?
6. Does it sound like Million Hexagons rather than generic startup copy?
